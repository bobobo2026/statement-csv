The real job is not “PDF magic”

For bookkeeping, the job is: get trustworthy rows into a spreadsheet or accounting import, reconcile opening and closing balances, and move on. You do not need a full OCR platform on months when every client already sends digitally issued PDFs.

Why subscriptions clash with statement volume

  • Burst usage — tax season and month-end are heavy; mid-month can be quiet. A flat seat charges for quiet weeks too.
  • Low page counts — many freelancers and small-practice bookkeepers only need on the order of tens of pages per month, not enterprise volume.
  • Minimum seats — tools built for firms often price for always-on access, not “pay when you convert.”
  • Switching cost — once templates and habits live inside one SaaS, canceling feels risky even when the math does not favor you.

We have seen public discussions (for example on bookkeeping forums) where people say they only convert roughly 25–35 pages a month and feel pushed into subscription minimums. Treat that as a pattern, not a universal statistic—your volume may differ.

What “good enough” looks like instead

  1. Pay for pages or stay free — until you truly need OCR and multi-user workflows.
  2. Balance verification — opening + deposits − withdrawals = closing before you import.
  3. Privacy — prefer tools that do not keep your bank PDFs on disk after the request.
  4. Honest limits — text-layer vs scan should be stated up front so you do not bounce after upload.

A simple decision rule

  • Mostly text-layer PDFs, low monthly pages → try a free converter first (StatementCSV).
  • Lots of phone photos / scanned packets → you need OCR; evaluate paid platforms for that job.
  • Busy only in tax season → prefer pay-per-page (when available) over a year-round seat.
