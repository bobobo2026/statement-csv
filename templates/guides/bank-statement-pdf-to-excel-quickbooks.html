Bank statement PDF to Excel for QuickBooks (CSV import steps)

QuickBooks does not turn a bank PDF into a clean transaction list by itself. Bookkeepers usually convert the statement to Excel or CSV first, verify the math, then upload. This page is a practical checklist—not tax or accounting advice.

Direct answer

To get a bank statement PDF into QuickBooks Online: (1) use a text-layer PDF from your bank, (2) convert it to CSV or Excel with a balance check, (3) upload via Banking → Upload transactions (or your region’s CSV import), (4) map Date / Description / Amount columns and review matches.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm text layer — highlight a date in the PDF. No selectable text = scan; StatementCSV cannot OCR yet.
  2. Convert — open the free converter, upload (max 15 MB), click Extract transactions.
  3. Balance check — require opening + deposits − withdrawals = closing before you trust the file.
  4. Download — Excel for filters; CSV for QuickBooks upload templates.
  5. Import — in QuickBooks Online, upload the file, map columns, and reconcile any unmatched rows.

FAQ (answer-engine style)

Can QuickBooks import a bank PDF directly?
Usually no for arbitrary statement PDFs. Convert to CSV/Excel first, then use Banking upload or a CSV template.
Excel or CSV for QuickBooks?
Both can work. CSV is the common upload format; Excel is better for cleaning columns before import.
What if the balance check fails?
A row may be missing or miscategorized. Fix in Excel before importing—do not force a bad file into QuickBooks.
Is StatementCSV free? Do you keep my PDF?
Free while billing is paused. No account. Parsed in RAM; not retained. See Privacy.
Does this work with Xero too?
Same idea: clean CSV/Excel first, then use Xero’s bank statement import and map columns.
