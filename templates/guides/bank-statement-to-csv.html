How to convert a bank statement PDF to CSV (with a balance check)

Bookkeepers and freelancers often need a spreadsheet of transactions—not a locked PDF. This guide walks through a practical path: extract rows, download CSV or Excel, and verify the math before you import into QuickBooks, Xero, or your own workbook.

What you need

  • A text-layer bank statement PDF (issued digitally by the bank, not a photo of paper).
  • About two minutes and a browser—no account required on StatementCSV.

Honest limit: scanned or photographed statements need OCR. StatementCSV does not do OCR yet. If you cannot select text in the PDF viewer, conversion will fail until OCR ships.

Step 1 — Confirm it is a text-layer PDF

  1. Open the statement in any PDF reader.
  2. Try to highlight a date or amount with the mouse.
  3. If you can select text, you are good. If the page behaves like an image, it is a scan—stop here for now.

Step 2 — Upload and extract

  1. Go to the free converter.
  2. Drop the PDF (max 15 MB) or click to choose a file.
  3. Click Extract transactions.

The tool parses the file in memory and builds a table of dates, descriptions, deposits, withdrawals, and balances when available. Uploads are not written to disk and are discarded after the request.

Step 3 — Read the balance check

Before you trust the CSV, check the summary box. A healthy parse aims for:

opening balance + deposits − withdrawals = closing balance

  • Pass — row totals line up with the statement’s opening and closing figures (within rounding).
  • Fail — a row may be missing or miscategorized; fix in Excel before importing to accounting software.
  • Unknown — the PDF did not expose clear opening/closing amounts; still download and reconcile manually.

This check is the main reason to prefer a dedicated statement converter over a generic “PDF to Excel” dump: generic tools rarely verify that the money still adds up.

Step 4 — Download CSV or Excel

  1. Preview the transaction table on the page.
  2. Click Download CSV for imports and scripts, or Download Excel for filters and formulas.
  3. Spot-check a few rows against the PDF (first day, last day, and one large transfer).

Step 5 — Import carefully

  • QuickBooks Online / Xero: use their bank CSV import templates; you may need to rename columns.
  • Excel / Google Sheets: filter by date, then match to invoices or receipts.
  • Bank layouts differ. Odd formats may need a few manual fixes—that is normal, not a “100% every bank” claim.
